# Flow state direction

Idea under consideration (2026-10-24): use Open-Motion to visualize psychological flow (Csikszentmihalyi's challenge-skill balance), or to help induce it. This file covers what the device can and cannot do here, which datasets exist, and a version that fits the event rules.

## What Open-Motion can contribute

- **It senses; it does not stimulate.** Open-Motion cannot induce anything. Flow is induced by task design: matching difficulty to skill. The classic paradigm is Tetris or mental arithmetic at three levels (boredom, flow, overload or frustration). A device can only close the loop by feeding a state estimate back into the task's difficulty.
- **Signals it gives:** cortical-depth BFI and BVI at 40 Hz, left and right, plus the systemic signals inside BFI: pulse (so heart rate and beat-to-beat variability) and respiration. Those systemic signals are Track 1 seed ideas.
- **No live human use this weekend.** Openwater's rules forbid placing the device on anyone, team members included. A loop driven by a real person's Open-Motion signal cannot run at this event. A loop driven by replayed data, synthetic data, or a flow phantom can.
- **Open-LIFU is out of scope for induction.** No ultrasound energy goes to any person at this event.

## What the literature says (mixed)

| Study | Measure | Flow finding |
| :- | :- | :- |
| Yoshida et al. 2014, Neurosci Lett, n=20, game flow vs boredom | fNIRS oxy-Hb, frontal | Oxy-Hb rose in left and right ventrolateral PFC during flow; tended to fall in boredom |
| Harmat et al. 2015, n=77, Tetris three levels (per review table, not read in full) | fNIRS, ECG, EDA | No association between frontal oxygenation and flow |
| Rácz et al. 2025, Sci Rep, n=24 usable, Tetris flow / boredom / frustration | HR, HR SD, SpO2, GSR, 4-channel frontal EEG, motion | HR: flow about equal to boredom, frustration much higher. HR variability lowest in flow (U-shape). Alpha and theta dominant in flow. Least motion in flow |
| Beiramvand et al. 2026, arXiv 2609.19737, n=45, adaptive Tetris | Muse-S and Emotiv Insight EEG | 97% accuracy with random CV, 75% leave-one-subject-out. Dataset release not stated |

Takeaways: the prefrontal hemodynamic evidence is inconsistent, while heart-rate features separated flow from frustration in the most recent public study. Open-Motion measures blood flow, not oxygenation, and a forehead placement mixes cortical and scalp blood. Any cortical "flow signal" from BFI is exploratory. Heart rate and its variability, extracted from BFI, are the better-supported bridge.

## A version that fits the rules

**Flow-adaptive task driven by a physiological flow estimate.**

1. **Model:** train a flow vs boredom vs frustration classifier on Rácz heart-rate features (mean HR, HR variability over windows), with leave-one-subject-out evaluation and a baseline. Report it honestly even if it's weak.
2. **Bridge:** build pulse and heart-rate extraction from Open-Motion BFI (Track 1), so the same features can come from the device. Validate on `sample_scan.csv` and any provided data.
3. **Loop:** a Tetris-style task whose speed adapts toward the estimated flow band. At the demo, drive it from replayed Open-Motion data, or live from a phantom whose flow the team modulates (if Openwater has a flow phantom). That uses the weekend station access.
4. **View:** a live display placing the current estimate on a challenge-skill map, with left and right BFI and BVI underneath and cortical channels marked exploratory.

What it claims: a working closed-loop pipeline, a cross-subject estimate of flow from heart-rate features with honest error bars, and a device-side feature extractor. What it does not claim: that it induced or detected flow in anyone this weekend. Showing that needs human participants and self-report (for example the Flow Short Scale), which needs IRB review after the event.

Track fit: Wildcard, or a Track 1 plus 3 mashup. It speaks directly to the event prompt.
